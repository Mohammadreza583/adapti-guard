STRONGEST DEFENSIBLE CONTRIBUTION
A controlled mixed attack/legitimate evaluation of a discrete L0–L3 runtime intervention policy that escalates on attack success and de-escalates on legitimate-task utility/cost feedback, compared against fixed levels under unified ASR/utility/defense-cost/reward metrics—without claiming uniqueness of escalate/de-escalate levels in general (SafeHarness) or open-world robustness.

RESEARCH GAP
Whether a discrete intervention-level controller with legitimate-task cost-triggered de-escalation improves the security–utility–cost frontier versus fixed levels AND versus privilege-degradation baselines (e.g., SafeHarness-style) under adaptive/black-box evolving attacks (AutoDojo-like), not only under a frozen synthetic stream.

RESEARCH QUESTION
Under controlled mixed attack/legitimate workloads, does a discrete L0–L3 runtime intervention policy with legitimate-task cost-triggered de-escalation improve the security–utility–cost tradeoff relative to fixed levels, and does that advantage survive adaptive black-box evolving attacks?

HYPOTHESES
H1 Under an identical mixed attack/legitimate protocol, the AG adaptive L0–L3 controller achieves a Pareto improvement (or distinct nondominated point) vs Fixed-L0..L3 on (ASR, utility, defense_cost).
H2 Legitimate-task cost-triggered de-escalation reduces over-defense utility loss relative to escalate-only or no-de-escalation ablations without increasing ASR beyond a pre-specified bound.
H3 Under AutoDojo-style adaptive black-box attacks, AG’s adaptive levels retain a measurable SUC advantage over the best fixed level; if not, evolving-attack claims must remain PARTIAL/LIMITED.
